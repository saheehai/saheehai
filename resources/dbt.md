DBT was built for people whose emotions come on fast, hit hard, and take a
long time to come down. If you have been told you are "too much," if small
things become crises, if you have hurt yourself or come close, or if your
relationships tend to swing between very close and blown up, this is the
one designed with you in mind.

The "dialectical" part is a fancy word for a plain idea: two things can be
true at once. You are doing the best you can, and you need to do better.
Accepting yourself as you are, and changing. DBT holds both.

## What it actually teaches

DBT is mostly skills, taught and practiced like any other skill. They fall
into four groups:

- **Mindfulness.** Noticing what is happening in you right now without
  immediately reacting to it. Everything else depends on this one.
- **Distress tolerance.** Getting through a terrible moment without making
  it worse. Concrete things: cold water on your face, intense exercise for a
  few minutes, paced breathing. They sound small. They work because they
  change your body's state, not because they change your mind.
- **Emotion regulation.** Understanding what an emotion is asking you to do,
  and deciding whether to do it. Reducing how often you end up flooded in
  the first place.
- **Interpersonal effectiveness.** Asking for what you need and saying no
  without either caving or exploding. Keeping the relationship and your
  self-respect at the same time.

## What a program looks like

Full DBT is more than weekly therapy. It usually means a one-on-one session,
a weekly skills group that runs like a class, and the option to call your
therapist between sessions for coaching when you are about to do something
you will regret. A full program runs six months to a year.

Not everyone needs all of that. Plenty of therapists use DBT skills inside
ordinary weekly therapy, and DBT skills groups on their own help a lot of
people. If someone offers you "DBT-informed" therapy, that is what they mean.

## Does it work?

Yes, with strong evidence. It was developed by Marsha Linehan for people
with borderline personality disorder and chronic suicidal thoughts, and it
cut self-harm and hospital stays in ways nothing else had. Since then it has
been shown to help with eating disorders, substance use, PTSD and
adolescents in crisis. The
[National Institute of Mental Health](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/psychotherapies)
and [NAMI](https://www.nami.org/about-mental-illness/treatments/psychotherapy/)
both summarize the research.

## What it costs

More than plain therapy if you do the full program, because there are more
hours in it. Insurance usually covers it. Community mental health centers
often run DBT skills groups at low cost or free, and they are frequently the
easiest way in. Ask for the group by name.

The skills themselves are not secret. The
[DBT Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets](https://www.guilford.com/books/DBT-Skills-Training-Handouts-and-Worksheets/Marsha-Linehan/9781572307810)
is the actual manual and is written for people using it, not just
clinicians.

## What to do next

- If your emotions regularly feel unsurvivable, tell a therapist that and
  ask about DBT. That sentence is enough. You do not need a diagnosis first.
- Ask your local community mental health center whether they run a skills
  group. Search [FindTreatment.gov](https://findtreatment.gov).
- If you are thinking about hurting yourself right now, this guide can wait.
  Call or text 988. Say what is happening. They will not overreact.
