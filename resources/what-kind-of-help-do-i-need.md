If you are not sure who to see, start with a therapist or your primary care
doctor. Both can help you figure out what you need next, and both are easier
and cheaper to get in front of than a psychiatrist.

## What each title means

- **Therapist or counselor.** A licensed professional who does talk therapy.
  In the US the licenses are usually LPC, LCSW, LMFT or LMHC. They cannot
  prescribe medication. Most mental health care starts here.
- **Psychologist.** Has a doctorate (PhD or PsyD). Does therapy and formal
  testing, for example for ADHD or learning differences. Cannot prescribe in
  most states.
- **Psychiatrist.** A medical doctor (MD or DO) who can prescribe and manage
  medication. Some do therapy; most focus on medication and see people less
  often.
- **Psychiatric nurse practitioner (PMHNP).** Can prescribe in most states,
  often with shorter waits and lower fees than a psychiatrist.
- **Peer support specialist.** Someone with lived experience, trained to
  support others. Not a clinician. Often free through community programs.

## When to start with which

- **You want to talk something through, or you are anxious, low, grieving,
  stuck.** A therapist.
- **You think medication might help, or a therapist has suggested it.** Your
  primary care doctor can prescribe many common medications and refer you on
  if it gets complicated. A psychiatrist or PMHNP for anything more involved.
- **You need a diagnosis for school or work accommodations.** A psychologist,
  for testing.
- **You cannot afford any of the above right now.** A community health center
  (search at [findahealthcenter.hrsa.gov](https://findahealthcenter.hrsa.gov))
  charges based on income and often has counselors on site. Peer support and
  warmlines are free. See our [Get help](/help) page.

## What a first appointment is like

The first session is mostly questions: what brought you in, what you have
tried, what your days look like. You do not need to have it figured out. It
is normal to see two or three therapists before one fits, and it is fine to
say so and move on.

## What to do next

- Decide whether you mainly want to talk, want medication, or need testing.
- Search [FindTreatment.gov](https://findtreatment.gov) or
  [Open Path Collective](https://openpathcollective.org) and filter by what
  you can pay.
- If you are in crisis, do not wait for an appointment. Call or text 988.
