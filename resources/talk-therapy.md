Talk therapy is a set of regular conversations with someone trained to
listen, notice patterns, and help you change what is not working. That is
the whole idea. Every other kind of therapy in this section is a particular
way of having those conversations.

## What actually happens

You sit down, in a room or on a video call, usually for fifty minutes, usually
once a week. The first session or two is mostly questions: what brought you
in, what your days look like, what you have already tried. After that it is
less structured than people expect. You talk about what is on your mind, and
the therapist helps you see it more clearly than you can on your own.

There is no test to pass and no right way to do it. Crying is normal. Not
crying is normal. Sitting in silence for a bit is normal too.

## Does it work?

Yes, for most people and most common problems. Decades of research show
psychotherapy helps with depression, anxiety, grief, relationship trouble
and stress, and that the effect is on par with medication for many of those,
with fewer side effects. The
[American Psychological Association](https://www.apa.org/topics/psychotherapy)
and the
[National Institute of Mental Health](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/psychotherapies)
both keep plain-language summaries of that evidence.

The single biggest predictor of whether it helps is not the method. It is
whether you feel understood by the person in front of you. If that is not
there after three or four sessions, it is fine to say so and try someone
else. Therapists expect this.

## How long it takes

It depends what you came for. Something specific, like a fear of driving or
a rough patch at work, might take eight to sixteen sessions. Longer patterns,
the kind you have carried since childhood, take longer. Many people go for a
few months, stop, and come back later when something new comes up. None of
that is failure.

## What it costs

With insurance in the US, expect a copay per session, often somewhere
between the price of a takeout meal and a tank of gas. Without insurance,
private rates vary a lot by city. Two things bring the price down:

- **Sliding scales.** Many therapists charge less if you earn less. You have
  to ask. [Open Path Collective](https://openpathcollective.org) lists
  therapists who commit to reduced rates.
- **Community health centers.** They charge by income and often have
  counselors on site. Search at
  [findahealthcenter.hrsa.gov](https://findahealthcenter.hrsa.gov).

Our [paying for care](/resources) guides go deeper on this.

## What to do next

- Read the other guides in this section if you want a sense of the main

  message two or three people. A short call to see if it feels right is
  normal and free.
- If you need someone tonight, not in three weeks, call or text 988, or go
