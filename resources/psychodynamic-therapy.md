Some problems are not really about the thing in front of you. You keep
ending up in the same kind of relationship. You get angry at your boss in a
way that is about someone else entirely. You sabotage things right when
they start to go well and you cannot say why. Psychodynamic therapy is for
that: the patterns that keep repeating, and the parts of you that run them
without asking.

It is the descendant of psychoanalysis, but the couch and the five days a
week are long gone. Today it looks like a weekly conversation that goes
deeper than most.

## What a session is like

Less structured than [CBT](/resources/cbt), and on purpose. You talk about
whatever is on your mind, and the therapist listens for what is underneath:
themes, contradictions, the thing you skipped past. They will ask about your
history, your family, your earliest memories of feeling the way you feel
now. They will also notice how you are with them in the room, because how
you are with them is usually how you are with everyone.

You may leave sessions feeling stirred up rather than tidy. That is common,
and it usually settles into something that feels like clarity.

## Who it helps most

People who have tried the practical approaches and found the same problem
comes back wearing a different outfit. People who want to understand
themselves, not just manage symptoms. Long-standing depression, trouble in
close relationships, a sense of emptiness or not knowing what you want,
grief that has not moved in years. Also people who simply find worksheets
alienating and want to be talked with, not coached.

It is a slower road. If you are in acute distress or need a specific fear
gone by spring, start with something more targeted and come back to this.

## Does it work?

Yes, and the effects tend to keep growing after therapy ends, which is
unusual. A widely cited
[review in the American Psychologist](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/amp-65-2-98.pdf)
found psychodynamic therapy as effective as other evidence-based approaches
for common conditions, with gains that continued to build at follow-up. The
[National Institute of Mental Health](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/psychotherapies)
lists it among the main evidence-based psychotherapies.

## How long it takes

Longer. Brief psychodynamic therapy exists and runs twelve to forty
sessions. Open-ended work can go a year or more. You and the therapist
decide together, and you can stop whenever you want. Many people find that
the length is the point: some things only shift once you trust someone for
a while.

## What it costs

Weekly sessions for a year add up, so cost matters more here. Three things
help:

- **Training clinics.** Psychoanalytic institutes and university psychology
  programs offer low-fee therapy with supervised trainees. The therapy is
  often excellent and the fees can be a fraction of private rates. Search
  for "psychoanalytic institute" or "psychology training clinic" plus your
  city.
- **Sliding scales.** Common among psychodynamic therapists. Ask.
- **Open Path Collective.** Filter for psychodynamic at
  [openpathcollective.org](https://openpathcollective.org).

## What to do next

- If the same thing keeps happening to you and you are tired of it, say
  exactly that to a therapist and ask whether psychodynamic work fits.
- Look up the nearest training clinic. It is the best-kept secret in
  affordable therapy.
- If you are in crisis, this is not the moment for a deeper timeline. Call
  or text 988.
