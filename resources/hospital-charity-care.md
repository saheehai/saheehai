If you got a bill from a nonprofit hospital in the United States, there is a
good chance the hospital has a written policy that would reduce or cancel
it based on your income. Federal law requires it. Most people are never told.
You have to ask, and this guide is how.

## Does this apply to my hospital?

Nearly six in ten US hospitals are nonprofit, and every nonprofit hospital
must have a financial assistance policy (often called "charity care") to
keep its tax exemption under
[Section 501(r)](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-hospitals-general-requirements-for-tax-exemption-under-section-501c3)
of the tax code. Public hospitals usually have one too. For-profit hospitals
are not required to, but many do.

To check: search the hospital's name plus "financial assistance policy". The
policy, a plain-language summary, and the application form must be posted on
the hospital's website.

## Do I qualify?

Every hospital sets its own line, but most forgive the whole bill for
households under 200% of the federal poverty level and discount it up to
300% or 400%. Some go higher. In 2026 the
[federal poverty guidelines](https://aspe.hhs.gov/topics/poverty-economic-mobility/poverty-guidelines)
put 200% at roughly $31,000 for one person and $64,000 for a family of four;
check the current figures.

Having insurance does not disqualify you. The policy applies to what you owe
after insurance.

## How do I apply?

1. **Ask for the application.** Call the hospital's billing office and say:
   "I would like to apply for financial assistance under your charity care
   policy." Write down the date and the name of the person you spoke to.
2. **Gather proof of income.** Usually your last tax return or two recent
   pay stubs, and a list of household members. If you have no income, say so
   in a short signed statement.
3. **Submit it and keep a copy.** Ask the billing office to put the account
   on hold while the application is reviewed. Under federal rules the
   hospital cannot send your bill to collections while an application is
   pending.
4. **Apply even if the bill is old.** Many policies allow applications for
   240 days after the first bill, and some hospitals will go back further.

## What if they say no or ignore me?

- Ask for the denial in writing and the reason.
- Ask whether an itemized bill was checked for errors. Duplicate charges and
  wrong codes are common.
- [Dollar For](https://dollarfor.org) is a nonprofit that will check your
  eligibility and complete the application with you, free.
- Texas: the state's
  [Office of the Attorney General](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/consumer-protection)
  takes complaints about billing practices.

## What to do next

- Find your hospital's financial assistance policy online.
- Call billing, use the sentence above, and note who you spoke to.
- If you want a hand, start at [dollarfor.org](https://dollarfor.org).
